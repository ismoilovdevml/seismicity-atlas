Magnitudes

  • Magnitude types — Mw, mb, Ms, ML and Md measure different waves, and some of them stop growing for large earthquakes.
  • Direct vs proxy Mw — Why the atlas, since v0.2, computes b only from moment magnitudes that were measured, not converted.

Completeness & b-value

  • Magnitude of completeness (Mc) — The size above which a catalogue misses almost nothing, and how it changes over time.
  • Frequency-magnitude distribution — How many earthquakes of each size: the basic chart behind Mc and b.
  • Gutenberg-Richter b-value — The slope that says how quickly large earthquakes become rarer than small ones.

Uncertainty & quality

  • 95 % interval and the bootstrap — How sure a b-value is, estimated by re-drawing the catalogue many times.
  • Quality codes A, B, C and insufficient — Why some cells show a b-value and most do not.
  • b-positive — A b-value from magnitude differences that is not fooled by missing small aftershocks.

Sequences

  • Omori-Utsu decay — How aftershock rates fall with time: K, c and p.
  • Aftershock productivity and Båth's law — How large the largest aftershock tends to be, and how many aftershocks to expect.

Space & tectonics

  • H3 cells and the sampling circle — Hexagons for display, a fixed circle for the statistics.
  • Plate-boundary classes — Subduction, ridges, transforms and rifts in PB2002, and their b-values.
  • Depth and slabs — Why shallow and deeper earthquakes are mapped separately.
  • Mw ≥ 6 rate — How often large earthquakes happened in a cell, per area and per year.

Data & caveats
