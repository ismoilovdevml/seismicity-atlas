95 % interval and the bootstrap

The 95 % interval shown with every b-value is the range that would cover the true b in 95 of 100 repeats of the measurement, and the atlas estimates it by the bootstrap: re-drawing the catalogue many times and re-computing b.

Interactive diagram · Real example from the atlas →

Simple explanation

A b-value from a few hundred earthquakes is uncertain: a slightly different set of earthquakes would have given a slightly different number.

We cannot rerun the Earth, but we can rerun the sample. The bootstrap draws a new catalogue of the same size from the real one, at random and with replacement (some events twice, some not at all), and computes b again. After many such draws, the middle 95 % of the b-values is the interval.

A narrow interval means the data pin b down well. A wide one means they do not, and the atlas then withholds b (see “Quality codes”).

An everyday picture

To learn how much a poll result could wobble, you could ask a new random group of the same size, many times. The bootstrap does this with the people you already asked, drawing them again at random.

Technical details

Procedure per cell and depth class: draw n events with replacement from the n events in the sampling circle; re-estimate the cell Mc (MAXC + 0.2) and the per-event thresholds; compute b with the Aki-Utsu estimator; repeat 1,000 times. The interval is the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of the replicates, so it includes the Mc uncertainty (Woessner & Wiemer 2005).

Why 1,000 replicates (1000 since release 0.5.0, 200 before): the interval bounds are themselves estimated from random draws, and near the A/B/C limits their scatter can change a cell's code. In a test cell with 150 events (88 above threshold), the lower and upper bounds varied between random seeds with a standard deviation of 0.012 and 0.024 at 200 replicates, and 0.005 and 0.009 at 1000 (tests/test_atlaslib.py).

Why not only the formula: the classical standard error of b, σ ≈ b/√n (Aki) or the Shi–Bolt form, assumes a known, fixed Mc. Here the threshold itself is estimated and the sample is modest, so the percentile bootstrap is the more honest summary. The cutoff plots in the cell details use the Shi–Bolt ±1.96 σ for speed.

Limits: the bootstrap reproduces sampling noise, not systematic errors (a wrong magnitude scale, missing events above Mc, mixing two populations). Overlapping intervals do not prove equality; non-overlapping intervals are a conservative sign of a difference, not a formal test (Marzocchi et al. 2020).

On the site: the interval bar on the b scale in the cell panel, the bracket after every b value, the interval width that sets the quality code and the two-row interval chart of the comparison view.
