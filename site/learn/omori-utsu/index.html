Omori-Utsu decay

The Omori-Utsu law describes how the rate of aftershocks falls with time after a mainshock, roughly as one over the elapsed time, with three numbers: K (productivity), c (an early delay) and p (how fast the decay is).

Simple explanation

Right after a large earthquake, aftershocks come quickly; a day later, much less often; a month later, rarely. The rate falls fast at first and then slowly, for months or years.

Fusakichi Omori noticed in the 1890s that the rate falls roughly as 1/t. Tokuji Utsu added the exponent p: with p near 1, ten times later means about ten times fewer aftershocks per day.

K sets how many aftershocks there are overall, p how quickly they die out, and c a short time at the start where the rate is flatter. With a global catalogue, the first hours are often missing, so c may not be measurable.

An everyday picture

It is like the ringing of a large bell: loud and busy at first, then fading, quickly at first and then more and more slowly, but never stopping all at once.

Technical details

Omori-Utsu rate of events above the completeness magnitude:λ(t) = K / (t + c)^pwith t in days. The expected number in [t₁, t₂] is K ∫ (t + c)^−p dt.

Fit in this atlas (scripts/sequences.py): maximum likelihood on ComCat events within max(100 km, L) of the mainshock, L = 10^(−2.44 + 0.59 M) km (Wells & Coppersmith 1994), for 0–365 days. Mcat is MAXC + 0.2 of days 10–365, never below 4.5. The fit keeps the early sequence by modelling the time-dependent completeness Mc(t) = max(Mcat, Mm − 4.5 − 0.75 log₁₀ t) (Helmstetter et al. 2006; Page et al. 2016), with the sequence's b-positive in that model.

Quality: A needs n ≥ 100, σp ≤ 0.10, 0.5 ≤ p ≤ 2 and no second event within 0.5 units of the mainshock; B needs n ≥ 50 and σp ≤ 0.20. c_resolved is false when c is below 0.001 day or has no standard error: a teleseismic catalogue misses the first hours, so c is not constrained, while K and p remain usable.

On the site: the decay charts and quality badges on the Sequences page. The rate points use log-spaced bins of the counts above Mcat.
